How to use AI to write your life story

You can use AI as a friendly writing partner for your life story. It can ask you questions, help you organize memories and tidy up your sentences. The memories and the voice stay yours, and you decide what goes in.

Step by step

1. Decide who it is for. Your grandchildren, your family, or just you? Knowing the reader helps you choose what to include.

2. Let AI interview you. Many people find a blank page hard, so ask for questions instead. Open a free assistant such as ChatGPT or Claude and type:

"I want to write about my childhood. Please ask me one question at a time about where I grew up, and wait for my answer before asking the next."

3. Answer in your own words. Type, or use the microphone on your phone or computer to speak. Rough and rambling is fine. Do not worry about spelling.

4. Build an outline. After a few sessions, ask:

"Here are my answers so far. Please suggest a simple chapter outline, in the order things happened: [paste your answers]"

5. Turn notes into a draft. Ask AI to keep your voice:

"Turn these notes into a short chapter in the first person. Use simple, warm language. Do not add any facts that I did not give you."

6. Edit until it sounds like you. Read it aloud. Change anything that does not sound like you, and fix any detail that is wrong. You can say, "Make this shorter," or "Make this sound more like how I talk."

7. Add the small details. Smells, songs, favorite foods and the names of old streets make a story feel alive. Ask:

"What details could I add to this paragraph to make it more vivid? Ask me questions to help me remember."

8. Save and share. Copy your chapters into a document you keep, and back it up. You can print it or share it with family when you are ready.

What to watch out for

Keep your story your own.

  • AI may fill gaps with details that sound nice but never happened. Always tell it not to invent facts, and check every sentence.
  • Check dates, names and places against photos, letters or family members.
  • Be careful with private details about other people, especially living relatives. Think about whether they would be comfortable with it being shared.
  • Avoid typing sensitive information such as account numbers, passwords or medical records into an AI tool.
  • If your story touches on legal or money matters, such as a will or property, confirm those details with a lawyer or the official source.

Take it one chapter at a time

You do not have to write the whole story at once. Fifteen minutes a day, one memory at a time, adds up faster than you might expect.
