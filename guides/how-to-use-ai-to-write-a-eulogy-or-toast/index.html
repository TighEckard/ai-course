How to Use AI to Write a Eulogy or Toast

You can use AI to write a eulogy or toast by giving it a few true stories and details about the person, then asking it to turn them into a short, warm draft that you edit until it sounds like you. AI will not know your loved one, so the memories have to come from you. What it does well is organize your thoughts and help with the blank page.

If you have ever stared at an empty screen the night before a funeral, wedding, or retirement party, this guide is for you. You do not need any technical skill. If you can send an email, you can do this.

Step by step

1. Gather your raw material first. Before you open any AI tool, jot down five to ten things in plain words. Think of the person's habits, a funny moment, a saying they repeated, and what they taught you. Rough notes are fine. Spelling does not matter.

2. Decide the basics. Know the occasion, how long you will speak, and the mood you want. Most people speak for two to five minutes, which is roughly 300 to 650 words read aloud at a calm pace.

3. Open a free AI chat tool. Tools such as ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini all work. Type your request just as you would explain it to a thoughtful friend.

4. Give it a clear prompt. Here is an example you can copy and change:

"Help me write a three-minute eulogy for my grandfather, Walter. He was a mail carrier for 31 years, loved fishing at Miller's Pond, told terrible puns, and always kept butterscotch candy in his coat pocket. I want it warm and gently funny, not too sad. Write it in a plain, spoken style, and leave a spot where I can add my own memory."

For a toast, try: "Write a 90-second toast for my sister's 50th birthday. She is generous, always late, and the best cook in our family. Keep it light and end by asking everyone to raise a glass."

5. Ask for changes. The first draft is only a start. Say things like "make it shorter," "add more humor," "less formal," or "remove the second paragraph." You can ask as many times as you like.

6. Put in your own words. Replace any line that does not sound like you. Add one real memory that only you could tell. This is the part people remember.

7. Read it out loud. Practice twice. Mark places to pause. Print it in large type so it is easy to read when your hands are shaking a little, which is normal.

What to watch out for

Check every fact. AI can invent details, including dates, names, places, and quotes, and it can sound very sure when it is wrong. Never let it add a fact you did not give it. Read every line and fix anything untrue.

Keep private details private. Avoid typing full names, addresses, medical information, or financial details into a chat tool. First names and general stories are enough.

Watch for clichés. Drafts can lean on phrases like "they will be dearly missed." If a line feels generic, replace it with something specific.

Ask a family member to read it. Someone close to the person can catch mistakes and may add a memory you forgot.

It is your tribute. AI is a helper, not an expert on your loved one. If a sentence does not feel right, trust yourself and change it.

A gentle note

Writing about someone you love can be emotional, and that is okay. Take breaks. You may find that the notes you gather in step one are the most meaningful part, and the AI simply helps you shape them.
