AI for Special Education Teachers: Prep Help With Privacy First

Special educators juggle detailed paperwork and tailored materials. AI can reduce the prep, as long as student privacy is protected.

Step by step

  1. Never enter student identifiers. No names, ID numbers or IEP text.
  2. Describe the need generally. For example, a reader needing simplified text.
  3. Ask for adapted materials. Visual schedules and checklists.
  4. Review with your team. Specialists and families.
  5. Follow your district. Policies differ.

What to expect

Expect visual supports, simplified texts and checklists, which still need your professional review.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not enter student data. Avoid using AI for legal documents.

A good next step

Share drafts with your team before using them.

Where AI helps most in special education

  • Breaking tasks into smaller steps.
  • Creating visual schedules and social stories to review.
  • Drafting goals and progress notes for you to edit.
  • Making simpler versions of texts.
  • Preparing meeting talking points.

Try it this week, step by step

  1. Choose one upcoming lesson. Note the grade, the standard and the time you have.
  2. Ask for a first draft. "Rewrite this 4th grade reading passage at a 2nd grade reading level, keep the main ideas and add three picture-supported comprehension questions."
  3. Ask for a simpler and a harder version. This helps you reach the whole room without writing three lessons.
  4. Check it against your classroom. Cut anything that does not match your students, materials or time.
  5. Save the prompt. Keep a running list of prompts that worked so next week is faster.

What to watch for

  • Never enter identifying information about students.
  • Keep legal documents and IEPs in approved systems; use AI only for general wording help.
  • Never paste student names, grades or personal details.
  • Follow your school or district policy on AI use.

A prompt you can copy

Type this into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini

Create a simple five-step visual schedule for a morning routine in a primary classroom, using short sentences and a suggested picture for each step.

Good to know. IEP language and legal compliance belong to qualified staff. Do not paste IEPs into chatbots.

Common questions

Can AI write IEP goals?

Do not use it for that without your district's approval.

Is it good for social stories?

It can draft one. Review for accuracy and sensitivity.

Can it write an IEP?

It can help with wording, but the plan must come from your team and the student data.

Is it private?

Do not share confidential records with an AI tool.

Can it make visuals?

It can suggest layouts. Use approved symbols or images.
