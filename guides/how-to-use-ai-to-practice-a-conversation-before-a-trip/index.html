How to use AI to practice a conversation before a trip

You can rehearse a trip conversation with an AI chat tool the same way you might rehearse with a patient friend. Tell it where you are going and who you will be talking to, and ask it to play that person. You get to practice ordering dinner, asking for directions or checking in at a hotel, and nobody is waiting behind you in line.

Here is how to set it up in a few minutes.

Step by step

1. Decide which situations worry you most. Think about the moments you would like to feel ready for. Common ones are the airport, the hotel desk, a restaurant, a taxi, a pharmacy or a market. Pick one to start.

2. Set the scene. Open an AI chat tool such as ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini and type:

"I am travelling to Italy next month and I speak very little Italian. Please pretend to be a waiter in a small restaurant in Rome. Use short, simple Italian and give the English meaning in brackets after each line. Wait for my reply each time."

3. Reply in your own words. Answer as you would on the trip, even if you only know a few words. If you are stuck, say: "I do not know how to say this. How would I say 'a table for two, please'?"

4. Ask for gentle feedback. After the practice chat, type:

"Please tell me what I did well, what I got wrong, and three phrases I should remember. Keep it friendly and simple."

5. Collect a small phrase list. Ask: "Give me ten useful phrases for this trip with the English meaning and an easy guide to saying them." Write them on a card or in the notes app on your phone.

6. Practice the tricky bits. Return to anything that went badly. Try: "Let us do that again, but this time the waiter speaks a little faster."

7. Rehearse the unexpected. Ask the AI to throw in a surprise, such as a delayed train, a wrong order or a closed museum. Handling small problems in practice makes real ones less scary.

8. Learn the local customs. Ask: "What are some polite customs I should know when eating out or greeting people in this country?" Use the answer as a starting point only.

What to watch out for

Good to know before you go

  • AI can be wrong. It may suggest a phrase that sounds odd or is too casual for the setting. Check important things with a phrasebook, a trusted travel site or someone who speaks the language.
  • Never rely on it for official facts. Entry rules, visas, vaccinations, opening hours, prices and train times change often. Confirm them with the official government website, your airline, your hotel or your travel agent.
  • Health and money. If you need medical advice for travelling, ask your doctor or pharmacist. For money questions such as cards and exchange, check with your bank.
  • Real people speak differently. Locals use slang, speak fast and have accents. Your practice partner is a warm-up, not the real thing, so expect to say "Could you repeat that, please?" and do not worry.
  • Protect your privacy. Do not type passport numbers, card numbers or your home address into a chat.

A small tip for the day of travel

Keep a few key phrases and your hotel address written down on paper, in case your phone battery runs out. A smile and a friendly "thank you" in the local language go a long way, and most people are glad you tried.
