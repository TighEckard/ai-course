How to use AI to learn a language

You can use an AI chat tool like ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini as a patient practice partner for a new language. Type in what you want to learn, ask it to quiz you, chat with you at your level, and explain your mistakes. It will not replace a good teacher or a course, but it never gets tired, never judges, and is available whenever you have ten spare minutes.

Here is a simple way to start.

Step by step

1. Tell the AI who you are and what you want. The more it knows, the better its help. Open any AI chat tool and type something like:

"I am a beginner learning Spanish. I am 62 and want to chat with my neighbours and travel to Mexico. Please teach me in short, friendly lessons and use simple English to explain."

2. Start with a small lesson. Ask for just a few words or phrases at a time. For example:

"Teach me five useful greetings in Spanish. Show the Spanish, how to say it in plain English letters, and what it means."

3. Ask for a quiz. Remembering is the hard part, so test yourself often:

"Quiz me on those five greetings, one at a time. Wait for my answer before telling me if I was right."

4. Practice a short chat. Once you know a few phrases, ask the AI to play a role:

"Pretend you are a friendly shopkeeper. Speak only simple Spanish, two short sentences at a time. After each of my replies, gently correct any mistakes and tell me why."

5. Ask why, as often as you like. If a rule confuses you, just say so:

"I do not understand why you used 'estoy' instead of 'soy'. Explain it like I am new to grammar, with two easy examples."

6. Build a little routine. Ten minutes a day usually beats one long session a week. At the end of each chat, type: "Summarize what I learned today in a short list I can review tomorrow." Paste that list into a note on your phone or a notebook.

7. Add real voices. Many AI tools can listen and speak. If yours has a microphone button, try saying your phrases out loud. Also listen to real speakers through radio, music or videos in your chosen language, so your ear gets used to natural speech.

What to watch out for

Good to know before you start

  • AI can be wrong. It sometimes gives a wrong word, a made-up rule or an odd phrase that a native speaker would never say. For anything that matters, such as a letter, a form or a sign, check with a teacher, a good dictionary or a native speaker.
  • Smaller languages are riskier. AI tends to do best with widely spoken languages. For less common ones, expect more mistakes and double-check more.
  • Accents and pronunciation. Spelling a word out in English letters is only a rough guide. Listen to recorded native speakers too.
  • Do not share private details. Skip your full name, address, passwords and bank details in any chat.
  • Ask it to correct you. Many AI tools are polite and may let mistakes slide. Say: "Please point out every mistake I make, even small ones."

Making it stick

Keep your lessons short, ask for repeats, and be kind to yourself. Mistakes are how everyone learns. If an answer seems strange, ask the AI, "Are you sure? Can you explain that another way?" It often catches its own slip when asked again, though not always, so keep a trusted dictionary handy.
