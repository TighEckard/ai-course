Read a Contract or Lease With AI

Contracts are written to be hard. AI can translate, but it cannot replace a lawyer.

Step by step

  1. Remove personal details.
  2. Ask for a summary.
  3. Ask what to question.
  4. Highlight fees and exit terms.
  5. Get advice.

What to expect

Expect a plain summary of key terms such as payment, term, renewal, fees and termination, and a list of clauses to question.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not rely on AI as the final word. It can miss details or misread a clause. Avoid pasting documents with names and numbers.

A good next step

For anything important, pay for a short attorney review or use a legal aid clinic.

How to use AI on a contract

  • Remove names and addresses first.
  • Paste one section at a time and ask for a plain-language summary.
  • Ask: "What are the deadlines, fees and ways to cancel?"
  • Ask for questions to put to the other party.

Review it, step by step

  1. Get the full text. Make sure you have every page.
  2. Summarize each section.
  3. Make a list of dates and money. Rent, deposits, late fees and renewal dates.
  4. Flag anything unclear. Highlight and ask for clarifications in writing.
  5. Talk to a professional for big decisions. A lawyer or a tenant rights group can check what matters.

A lease example

  1. Paste a section. "Here is the section titled Termination and Renewal from my lease. Explain it in plain English and tell me the dates and fees."
  2. Possible answer. The lease renews automatically for another year unless you give written notice 60 days before it ends, and there is a $150 fee if you leave early.
  3. What you do. Put the 60-day date on your calendar, then ask the landlord to confirm in writing.

What to look for in any contract

  • Dates: when it starts, ends and renews.
  • Money: total cost, fees, deposits and penalties.
  • Your duties and theirs.
  • How to cancel and how much notice is required.
  • Automatic renewals.
  • What happens if there is a dispute.

Where AI helps and where it cannot

  • It can translate legal language into plain words.
  • It can create a list of questions to ask.
  • It can compare two versions to show what changed.
  • It cannot know your state's laws as a local lawyer does.
  • It may miss subtle clauses, so use it as a first read, not the final word.

When to get a professional

  • Large amounts of money or a long commitment.
  • Buying or selling property.
  • Anything involving a business sale, a will or a trust.
  • A dispute that has already started.

A prompt you can copy

Type this into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini

Summarize this lease clause in plain words and tell me what questions to ask my landlord: [paste].

Common questions

Is it safe to paste?

Remove names and numbers.

Can AI find hidden fees?

It can point out areas to question.

Can AI replace a lawyer?

No. It helps you understand and prepare.

Is it safe to paste a contract?

Remove sensitive details first.

What if it says something is illegal?

Verify with local resources before acting.

Is it safe to paste a lease?

Remove names, addresses and account numbers first.

Where can I get free legal help?

Look for local legal aid, a tenant union or a bar association referral line.
