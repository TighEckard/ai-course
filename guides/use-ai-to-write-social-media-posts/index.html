Use AI to Write Social Media Posts for Your Business

Posting regularly helps local customers find you, but ideas run out fast. AI can give you a month of post ideas and first drafts to match your business.

Step by step

  1. Describe your business in three sentences. Say what you sell, who buys it, and what makes you different.
  2. Ask for a calendar. Request 12 post ideas for the month, mixing tips, behind the scenes, and offers.
  3. Write posts one at a time. Pick an idea and ask for a short post in your voice.
  4. Add real photos. Use your own pictures of your work, not stock images, so posts look genuine.
  5. Schedule and check. Read each post for accuracy before it goes live.

What to expect

Expect a month of post ideas and first drafts in a consistent voice.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not fabricate reviews or testimonials. Avoid posting stock-sounding text with no real photos.

A good next step

Take five real photos this week and write captions with AI.

Before you post

  • Pick one or two platforms where your customers or friends actually are.
  • Decide on three themes, such as helpful tips, behind the scenes and offers.
  • Collect a few real photos. Real photos beat polished graphics.

A week of posts, step by step

  1. Describe your business and audience. Two or three sentences.
  2. Ask for seven post ideas. One per day, mixing the themes.
  3. Pick four and expand them. Ask for a caption of 40 to 80 words with a friendly tone.
  4. Add your own detail. A name, a place, a number or a story makes it yours.
  5. Schedule or post. Use the platform's scheduling tool if it has one.
  6. Look at what worked. Note which post got the most comments and make more like it.

Do and do not

  • Do ask for a clear call to action such as "call us" or "reply with your question."
  • Do keep hashtags to a few relevant ones.
  • Do not post made-up testimonials or fake before-and-after photos.
  • Do not paste private customer details.

A prompt you can copy

Type this into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini

I own a [business] in [town]. Our customers are [who]. Give me 12 Facebook post ideas for next month, with a one-line description of each. Mix helpful tips, behind the scenes, customer stories and two gentle offers.

Good to know. Do not invent reviews or customer quotes. Only use real ones.

Common questions

How often should I post?

Two or three times a week is plenty for most small businesses. Consistency matters more than volume.

What if the post sounds too salesy?

Ask for a friendlier version that teaches something first and mentions your business last.

How often should I post?

Two or three times a week that you can keep up beats daily posting that fizzles.

Can AI make the images?

It can help with ideas, but real photos tend to build more trust.

Will it sound like everyone else?

Add specifics from your own life or business to stand out.
