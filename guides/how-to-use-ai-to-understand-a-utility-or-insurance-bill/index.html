How to use AI to understand a utility or insurance bill

General help only. This is not medical, legal or financial advice. Confirm important details with a qualified professional or an official source.

You can use an AI assistant to explain a confusing bill in plain English. Type or paste in the wording from the bill, ask it to translate each line, and ask it what questions to put to the company. It will not replace the company or a professional, but it is a patient helper that never gets tired of "what does this mean?"

Here is a simple way to do it.

Step 1: Gather your bill and remove private details

Have the bill in front of you. Before you paste anything into an AI tool, cover or delete your account number, full address, birth date, and anything else personal. The AI does not need those to explain the charges.

Step 2: Type in the confusing part

Copy only the lines you do not understand. If you are on paper, simply type them out. Short and specific works best.

Step 3: Ask for a plain English explanation

Try a prompt like this:

"I am not an expert. Here are lines from my electric bill: 'Delivery charge, supply charge, fuel adjustment, regulatory fee.' Please explain what each one usually means in simple words, as if you were talking to a friend."

Notice that you told the AI who you are and how you want the answer. That makes a big difference.

Step 4: Ask what looks different from last time

If you have last month's bill, type a few numbers from both and ask:

"Last month my gas bill was $64 and this month it is $97. Here are the line items for each. What could explain the jump? Give me a few common possibilities."

The AI can suggest ordinary reasons, such as colder weather, a longer billing period, or a rate change. These are possibilities, not facts about your account.

Step 5: For insurance, ask about the words

Insurance bills and notices are full of terms like premium, deductible, and coverage period. Ask:

"Please explain these insurance terms in plain English and tell me which ones affect how much I pay each month: premium, deductible, co-pay, renewal."

Step 6: Make a list of questions to call about

Finish by asking:

"Based on this, write me a short list of questions to ask the company when I call, and a polite way to start the conversation."

Write the answers down during the call, along with the date and the name of the person you spoke to.

What to watch out for

  • AI can be wrong. It does not see your account, and it may guess. Treat every answer as a starting point, not the final word.
  • Rates and rules differ by company and state. The AI gives general explanations. Your provider's own website, bill, or customer service line is the real source.
  • Never share personal details. Skip account numbers, passwords, and Social Security numbers.
  • Confirm before you act. If a charge seems wrong, call the company or check its official site before disputing or paying. For questions about coverage or a claim, ask your insurance agent or the company directly. For legal or financial decisions, talk to a qualified professional, such as a lawyer or your bank.
  • Be wary of "helpful" strangers. If anyone contacts you out of the blue about your bill, hang up and call the number printed on the bill.

Keep practicing

The first time feels strange, and that is normal. After two or three tries, most people find it easy to ask follow-up questions like "Can you say that more simply?" or "What should I ask next?"
