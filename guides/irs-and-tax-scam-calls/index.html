IRS and Tax Scam Calls: How to Tell They Are Fake

Scammers love tax season. A real tax agency does not threaten arrest by phone or demand gift cards. Knowing a few facts keeps you calm.

Step by step

  1. Hang up. Do not argue or give any details.
  2. Know what is real. The IRS generally starts with a letter by mail.
  3. Never pay by gift card. Or wire or crypto.
  4. Check your account yourself. Use the official website you typed in.
  5. Report it. At reportfraud.ftc.gov and the tax agency's site.

What to expect

Expect callers who sound official, give a badge number and demand payment right now. They may threaten arrest, a lawsuit or loss of your license. Real tax agencies mail letters first and offer ways to appeal.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not call back a number left on a voicemail, give any digits of your Social Security number, or pay with gift cards, wire transfers or cryptocurrency. Do not be embarrassed to hang up. Do not click links in texts that claim to be about a refund.

A good next step

Look up the agency's real phone number yourself and call to ask whether you owe anything. Then report the call to the FTC.

A prompt you can copy

Type this into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini

I got a voicemail saying I owe back taxes and will be arrested today unless I call back. List the signs this is a scam and what I should do next.

Good to know. Scammers can fake the caller ID. A number that looks official can still be a scammer.
