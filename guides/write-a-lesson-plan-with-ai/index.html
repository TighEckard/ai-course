Write a Lesson Plan With AI

A lesson plan draft can take a minute instead of an hour. The thinking about what your students need is still yours.

Step by step

  1. State the objective. Say what students should be able to do by the end.
  2. List constraints. Include time, materials and any standards you must cover.
  3. Ask for the structure. Request opening, instruction, practice and closing.
  4. Request checks for understanding. Ask for two or three questions you can ask mid-lesson.
  5. Adapt it. Change examples to fit your students and your classroom.

What to expect

Expect a structured first draft.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not use without review. Avoid mismatching standards.

A good next step

Teach it, then note what to change.

What to tell the assistant

  • Grade level and subject.
  • The standard or learning goal.
  • How long the lesson is.
  • Materials you have and do not have.
  • What students already know and where they struggle.

Build a plan, step by step

  1. Start with the goal. "By the end of class, students can ..."
  2. Ask for a structure. Hook, mini-lesson, guided practice, independent practice, check for understanding.
  3. Add differentiation. "Add support for students who are behind and a challenge for those ahead."
  4. Ask for a quick check. "Write three questions I can use to see who got it."
  5. Revise. Cut anything you cannot do in the time you have.

A sample request

  1. Prompt. "Plan a 45-minute 5th grade lesson on fractions with unlike denominators. Students know equivalent fractions. Include a hook, two practice problems, and an exit ticket."
  2. Review. Check the math, timing and reading level before you use it.

A complete example

  1. Request. "Create a 50-minute 8th grade lesson on evaluating online sources. Students have laptops. Include a 5-minute hook, a 15-minute mini-lesson, 20 minutes of practice and a 5-minute exit ticket. Add support and extension ideas."
  2. What to expect. A timed outline, a list of sample websites to compare (which you should replace with real ones you have vetted), three discussion questions and an exit ticket.
  3. What to fix. Replace placeholder sources, adjust timing and match vocabulary to your students.

Making it your own

  • Add your routines, such as how students move and share.
  • Add local examples and student interests.
  • Insert a quick check for understanding every ten minutes.
  • Prepare materials before you teach, not after.

Checking quality

  • Does each activity match the learning goal?
  • Is the time realistic?
  • Are there ways for every student to participate?
  • Are facts and sources accurate?
  • Is there a way to see who understood?

A prompt you can copy

Type this into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini

Write a lesson plan for grade [x] on [topic], 50 minutes. Objective: students will be able to [skill]. Include materials, steps with timing, three questions to check understanding, and one extension for fast finishers.

Good to know. Always check facts and age-appropriateness before using AI content with students.

Common questions

Will it match my standards?

Name the standards yourself and verify the match.

Can it write a unit?

Yes, but build it lesson by lesson so you can steer.

Can it match my state standards?

You can paste the standard text so the plan is aligned. Always check it yourself.

Will every lesson sound the same?

Not if you give specifics about your class and ask for variations.

Can it write a sub plan?

Yes. Give it the schedule and routines.

How many lessons should I plan at once?

Draft a week in one sitting and refine each day.

Can it align to standards?

Paste the exact standard text and ask it to align, then verify.
