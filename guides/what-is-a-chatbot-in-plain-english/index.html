What is a chatbot, in plain English

A chatbot is a computer program you talk to by typing, much like sending a text message. You ask a question or make a request in everyday words, and it writes back. Some chatbots answer simple questions on a company website. Newer ones, such as ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude, can explain ideas, draft emails, and help you plan things. You do not need any technical skill to use one.

How a chatbot works, simply

Modern chatbots have read an enormous amount of writing. When you type something, they predict a helpful reply based on patterns in that writing. They are not looking up a single perfect answer, and they are not a person. Think of a very well-read assistant who writes quickly but sometimes gets things wrong.

How to try one today

  1. Pick a chatbot. ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude all have free versions. Open any one in your web browser and create a free account if asked.

  2. Find the message box. It sits at the bottom of the screen, like a text message field. Click in it.

  3. Type a plain request. Write as you would speak to a helpful neighbor. For example:

"I am new to this. In three short sentences, explain what a chatbot is, and then ask me what I would like help with."

  1. Read the reply and follow up. You can answer it or ask for changes. Try: "Please explain that again, more simply." There is no penalty for asking again.

  2. Give it more detail for better results. Compare "Write a thank-you note" with this:

"Write a warm, short thank-you note to my neighbor Carol, who watered my plants while I was away for a week."

  1. Check the result before you use it. Read it through, fix anything that sounds off, and only then copy it somewhere else.

Things people use chatbots for

  • Drafting and polishing emails or letters
  • Explaining a confusing word or a news story
  • Planning a trip, a menu or a weekly schedule
  • Suggesting gift ideas or party themes
  • Practicing a new language

What to watch out for

Keep these in mind

  • Chatbots can be wrong. They sometimes state things confidently that are not true. Never assume a reply is correct just because it sounds sure of itself.
  • Health, legal, money and Medicare questions. A chatbot can give general background, but it is not a doctor, lawyer, bank or official source. Confirm anything important with a qualified professional or the official website or office.
  • Protect your private details. Do not type passwords, bank or card numbers, or your Social Security number into a chatbot.
  • It is not a person. It may sound friendly and caring, but it is a program. Be careful with any chat that urges you to act quickly or send money.
  • Check the source. Only use the official website or app for the chatbot you choose. Lookalike sites exist.

Common questions

Do I have to pay? Free versions exist, though they may have limits. You can learn a great deal without paying anything.

Will I break something? No. If a reply is not useful, close the window or start a new chat.

Is it okay to ask silly questions? Yes. The chatbot does not mind, and practice is how everyone gets comfortable.
