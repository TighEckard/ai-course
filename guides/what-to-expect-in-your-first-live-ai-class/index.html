What to Expect in Your First Live AI Class (Step by Step)

Joining a live class for the first time can feel intimidating, especially if you have never used Zoom or ChatGPT. Here is exactly what happens, so you know what to expect.

Before class

  1. Reserve a seat. You choose a time and enter your first name and email. No card is needed for the first class.
  2. Look for the email. You will receive the class details and the Zoom link. Check your spam folder if you do not see it.
  3. Test your device. A phone, tablet or computer with a speaker and camera works. Open the Zoom link a few minutes early so there are no surprises.
  4. Open a chatbot if you like. If you want to follow along, have ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini ready. You can also just watch the first time.

During class

Riside classes are about an hour, led live by Holden Eckard, in groups of 12 or fewer. Here is the usual flow.

  • A friendly start. Everyone says hello. You can leave your camera off if you prefer.
  • One idea at a time. The teacher shows each step on screen, slowly, using everyday examples.
  • Your questions. You can unmute and ask out loud or type in the chat. There is no such thing as a silly question.
  • Hands-on practice. You try the idea yourself on something from your own life.

After class

If you enjoy it, you can continue with membership at $46 a month, which includes a live class every week, and every class is recorded for members. You can cancel any time, from your PayPal account or by email. If it is not for you, there is nothing to cancel because the first class is free and no card is needed.

Common worries

What if I cannot figure out Zoom?

Most people manage by tapping the link in their email. Our guide on video calling with Zoom, FaceTime and WhatsApp walks through it. You can also email us before class.

What if I am the slowest person there?

That is common and fine. Small groups mean the teacher can go at a pace that works, and recordings let you review.

Will I have to share personal information?

No. You never need to share passwords, bank details or private records. Practice uses made-up or general examples.

Do I need to speak?

No. You can listen, type in the chat, or speak when you feel ready.
