How to use AI to plan a community event

An AI chat tool, such as ChatGPT or Claude, can help you plan a community event by building checklists, schedules, invitations, and task lists from a few details you give it. It will not run the event or know your local rules, but it is very good at helping you think of everything, in order, so nothing gets forgotten.

Whether it is a bake sale, a neighborhood picnic, a fundraiser, or a talk at the library, here is a simple way to begin.

Step by step

1. Write down the basics. Note the type of event, the date, the place, roughly how many people, your budget, and how many helpers you have. Rough numbers are fine.

2. Ask for a planning checklist. Open an AI chat tool and try something like this:

"I am helping plan a fall community potluck for about 60 neighbors on Saturday, November 14, from 4 to 7 p.m. at our community hall. We have about 8 volunteers and a budget of $150. Please give me a week-by-week planning checklist starting six weeks before the event, in plain language."

3. Ask for a day-of schedule. For example: "Now make an hour-by-hour schedule for the day itself, including setup, welcome, food, activities, and cleanup. Suggest who should do each job."

4. Divide the jobs. Ask, "Turn this into a list of volunteer roles, with a short description of each job." You can then hand these out to your helpers.

5. Draft the invitation. Try: "Write a short, friendly invitation I can post on our notice board and send by email. Include the date, time, place, what to bring, and who to contact." Then fill in your real details.

6. Plan for problems. Ask: "What could go wrong at an event like this, and what is a simple backup plan for each?" Weather, a no-show volunteer, and too much or too little food are common ones.

7. Brainstorm. Ask for ideas for activities, themes, or ways to welcome new faces, and pick the ones you like.

8. Make a thank-you note. After the event, ask the AI to draft a warm thank-you message to your volunteers and attendees.

What to watch out for

Before you rely on the plan:

  • AI can be wrong or out of date. Treat its answers as a starting point, not a final plan. It does not know your venue, your town, or your group.
  • Check rules and permits yourself. Rules about food, alcohol, insurance, street closures, and fundraising differ from place to place. Confirm with your local council, venue, or an official source, and ask a lawyer if you are unsure about legal questions.
  • Check the numbers. Costs, quantities, and timings it suggests are estimates. Compare them to real quotes and your own experience.
  • Think about safety and access. Allergies, accessibility, first aid, and supervision need a real person's judgment. If there is a medical question, ask a doctor or your local health authority.
  • Protect privacy. Do not paste in guest lists with phone numbers, addresses, or health details.

One last tip

Save your best prompt and the final checklist. Next year, paste them back in and say, "Update this plan for next year, and here is what we learned." Planning gets faster each time, and the AI can help you remember what worked.
