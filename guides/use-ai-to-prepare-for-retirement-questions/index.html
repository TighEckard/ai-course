How to use AI to prepare for retirement questions (general info only)

General help only. This is not medical, legal or financial advice. Confirm important details with a qualified professional or an official source.

You can use AI as a patient helper that explains retirement terms in plain English and helps you build a list of good questions. It is not a financial advisor, a lawyer or a doctor, and it can be wrong, so think of it as a way to get ready for the real conversation, not a replacement for it.

Many people feel stuck because they do not know what to ask. AI is good at that first step. Here is how to do it.

Step 1: Open a free AI chat tool

Pick a chat assistant such as Claude or ChatGPT. You type a message in a box, and it types back. There is nothing to install if you use it in your web browser.

Step 2: Ask it to explain a word you do not understand

Start small. Ask about one term at a time.

Example prompt: "Explain what a required minimum distribution is, in plain English, as if I have never heard the term. Keep it under 150 words."

If the answer is confusing, type "Explain that again with a simple everyday example."

Step 3: Ask for a list of questions, not answers

This is the most useful trick. Tell the AI who you are and ask what to bring up with a professional.

Example prompt: "I am 62 and thinking about retiring in three years. I have a workplace retirement account and a small savings account. Please give me 10 general questions I should ask a financial advisor. Do not give me advice, just questions."

Step 4: Ask what documents to gather

Example prompt: "What papers and account statements should I gather before meeting with a financial planner about retirement? Give me a simple checklist."

You can copy the list into a notebook and tick items off as you find them.

Step 5: Practice the conversation

Example prompt: "Pretend you are a friendly advisor and I am a first-time client. Ask me five simple questions so I can practice explaining my situation."

This helps you feel calm before a real appointment.

Step 6: Write down what you learned and check it

Ask the AI to summarize what you covered, then take that summary to a qualified person. For anything about your own money, taxes, Social Security or Medicare, confirm with your bank, a licensed advisor, or the official source, such as ssa.gov and medicare.gov.

What to watch out for

  • AI can be confidently wrong. It may mix up rules, use old numbers, or invent details. Never treat an answer as final.
  • Rules change and differ by location. Tax, Social Security and Medicare rules are updated, and some depend on where you live.
  • Keep private details private. Do not type account numbers, passwords, your Social Security number or your full birth date into a chat.
  • It does not know your full situation. General information is not personal advice.
  • Confirm the big things. For money, legal and health decisions, check with a licensed professional or an official source before you act.

A calmer way to start

Retirement questions feel smaller once they are written down. AI can help you get there in an afternoon, then your advisor, bank or doctor can help with the answers that matter.
