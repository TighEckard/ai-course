How to use AI to write a volunteer or club newsletter

You can use an AI chat tool, such as ChatGPT or Claude, to turn your rough notes into a friendly newsletter draft in a few minutes. You type in what happened and what is coming up, ask for a draft, and then you read it, fix it, and make it sound like your group. The AI does the first draft. You stay the editor.

If you have ever stared at a blank page the night before a newsletter was due, this can take a lot of the stress out of it.

Step by step

1. Gather your raw material. Jot down the news in any order: past events, upcoming dates, thank-yous, reminders, and anything new members should know. Messy notes are fine.

2. Decide who is reading. Is it members, volunteers, families, or neighbors? Tell the AI, because it changes the tone.

3. Open an AI chat tool and paste in your notes with a clear request. Here is an example you can copy and change:

"I run the newsletter for the Maple Street Garden Club. Please write a friendly, one-page newsletter for our 40 members. Keep the tone warm and simple, with short paragraphs. Include these items: (1) Our fall plant swap is Saturday, November 7, 10 a.m. to noon, at the community hall. (2) Thank you to Joan and Pedro for running the spring sale. (3) Dues reminder: due by December 1. (4) We need two volunteers to bring snacks in November. Add a short subject line and end with a sign-off from the committee."

4. Read the draft slowly. Check every name, date, time, place, and amount against your own notes. This is the most important step.

5. Ask for changes in plain words. You can say, "Make it shorter," "Make it sound less formal," or "Add a friendly opening about the autumn weather." You can keep going back and forth as many times as you like.

6. Add your own touch. A real photo, a sentence about a funny moment, or a personal note from you is what makes members feel the newsletter is yours.

7. Save what worked. When you get a version you like, copy your prompt into a document. Next month, paste it in again with new notes, and you will have a starting point in seconds.

Handy extras

  • Ask for three subject line ideas and pick the best one.
  • Ask it to turn the newsletter into a short email, or a few lines for a notice board.
  • Ask it to check your own draft for spelling and clarity. Paste it in and say, "Please tidy this up but keep my voice."
  • Ask for a simple layout, such as headings and bullet points for dates.

What to watch out for

Before you hit send:

  • AI can get facts wrong or make things up. It does not know your group. Check every date, time, address, name, and number yourself.
  • Do not paste private details. Leave out phone numbers, home addresses, health news, or anything a member has not agreed to share.
  • Be careful with health, legal, or money advice. If your newsletter mentions any of these, keep it general and point readers to a doctor, lawyer, bank, or official source to confirm.
  • Do not invent quotes. If you want a member's words in the newsletter, use what they actually said, with their permission.
  • Read it out loud. If it does not sound like you, change it. Members like to hear a real voice.

A small habit that helps

Keep a running "newsletter notes" page all month. Whenever something happens, add a line. When it is time to write, paste the whole page into the AI and ask for a draft. You will never start from nothing again.
