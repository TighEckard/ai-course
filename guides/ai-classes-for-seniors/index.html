AI Classes for Seniors: How to Find a Good One (Free and Paid)

If you are over 60 and want to learn ChatGPT or other AI tools, you have more choices than you might think. This guide compares the main places to learn, what a good beginner class should include, and the red flags to avoid.

Where older adults can learn AI

There is no single best place. The right one depends on whether you like learning in person, at home, alone or with others. Here are the common options.

  • Public libraries. Many libraries offer free computer and technology help. Ask the front desk whether they run any classes on AI tools or can book a one-on-one session. Offerings differ a lot from one library to the next.
  • Senior centers and community organizations. Some host technology workshops. These are often free or low cost and a good way to meet neighbors. You can find your local Area Agency on Aging with the Eldercare Locator.
  • Community colleges and adult education. Look for continuing education or non-credit courses. Check the course description to see whether it is current, because AI tools change quickly.
  • Free video tutorials. You can learn a lot on your own, but videos do not answer your questions and can drift out of date. Our guide on online AI class versus YouTube videos explains the tradeoffs.
  • Live online classes. A live class on Zoom lets you ask questions out loud and join from home, which helps if travel, weather or mobility makes in-person classes hard.

What a good beginner AI class should include

  1. It starts from zero. You should not need to know what a prompt is or how to install anything. The teacher should walk through opening the tool on your phone or computer.
  2. It is small enough for questions. In a group of 12 or fewer, everyone can speak. In a group of 200, most people stay silent and get lost.
  3. It uses your real life. The best practice is on things you actually do: writing an email, reading a confusing letter, planning a trip, checking a suspicious message.
  4. It teaches safety first. A good class explains what never to type into a chatbot, how scams use AI voices and fake messages, and how to double check important answers.
  5. It does not sell you anything else. You should never be asked for passwords, bank details or gift cards, and the price should be clear before you pay.

Red flags to watch for

  • Pressure to pay today. A real class lets you try before you commit.
  • Requests for passwords or account access. A teacher never needs these.
  • Promises that AI will make you rich. Beginner classes teach practical skills, not get-rich schemes.
  • Hidden fees. Ask what the total cost is each month and how to cancel.

Free ways to start this week

You do not have to pay anything to begin. ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini all offer free versions. Open one on your phone, and try a question like this:

Copy and paste

I am new to AI. Explain in three short sentences what you can help me with, and suggest three simple things I could ask you to do this week.

If the answer is helpful, ask a follow-up. If it is confusing, say so and ask for a simpler version. Our guide on how to write a good AI prompt shows an easy pattern for better answers.

Common questions

Are AI classes hard for older adults?

No. If you can open a web page or send a text message, you have the skills you need. A good class goes at the pace of the slowest learner in the room.

Do I need a new computer or phone?

No. Most phones, tablets and computers from the last several years work. You need internet and, for a live class, a camera and speaker.

Is it safe for beginners to use ChatGPT?

Yes, if you follow a few rules, mainly never typing passwords, bank details or full medical records. Read Is AI safe to use? for the full list.

What if I fall behind?

Ask questions as often as you like. In a small group, nobody minds, and recordings let you rewatch anything.
