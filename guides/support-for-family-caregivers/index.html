Support for Family Caregivers: Organize, Ask for Help and Rest

Caregiving is demanding and often unseen. Organization and asking for help protect both of you.

Step by step

  1. Write down the tasks. Medicines, meals, appointments, bills.
  2. Share tasks by name. Ask each person for one specific job.
  3. Find respite. Adult day programs or volunteers can give you a break.
  4. Keep one binder. Contacts, medicines and doctors in one place.
  5. Protect your own health. Keep your own appointments and sleep.

What to expect

Expect help organizing tasks, wording requests for help and ideas for short breaks that actually fit your day.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not try to do everything alone. Avoid hiding stress from the doctor. Do not ignore your own health.

A good next step

Call your Area Agency on Aging and ask about respite and caregiver support programs.

A prompt you can copy

Type this into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini

I care for my husband, who has early memory loss. Help me make a weekly task list I can share with family, with a column for who does what and a spot for my own rest time.

Good to know. Your Area Agency on Aging can connect you with local help. The Eldercare Locator at eldercare.acl.gov is a good starting place.

Common questions

Is it selfish to take a break?

No. Rest helps you keep caring.

Where do I find caregiver groups?

Local aging agencies, hospitals and online groups.
